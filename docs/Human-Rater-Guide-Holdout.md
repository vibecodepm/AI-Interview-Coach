# Simple Guide for the Second Human Rater

## Why you are rating these answers

This project helps Product Manager candidates practise interview questions. We are checking whether the AI gives fair and useful feedback on interview answers.

You will review 52 question-and-answer examples. Your ratings will be used to compare human judgement with the AI's judgement. There are no right answers; we want your honest judgement.

## What to do

For each example:

1. Read the interview question.
2. Read the candidate's answer.
3. Give a score from **0 to 5** for each of the four areas below.
4. Judge only what the answer actually says. Do not guess missing facts.
5. Rate each example independently. Do not look at another person's ratings.

Half-point scores are allowed, such as 2.5 or 4.5.

## The four areas

### 1. Relevance — Did the answer answer the question?

- **0:** Does not answer the question at all.
- **1:** Mostly unrelated or only vaguely connected.
- **2:** Partly answers the question but misses important parts.
- **3:** Answers the question adequately.
- **4:** Directly and clearly answers the question.
- **5:** Answers it exceptionally well and stays focused throughout.

### 2. Structure — Is the answer easy to follow?

- **0:** Impossible to follow.
- **1:** Very confusing or just a list of disconnected points.
- **2:** Some order, but important parts are unclear or missing.
- **3:** Generally understandable with a reasonable flow.
- **4:** Clear beginning, middle, and end.
- **5:** Very clear, focused, and logically organised.

### 3. Specificity — Does it explain what the candidate actually did?

- **0:** No usable details.
- **1:** Very general statements with almost no personal actions.
- **2:** Some details, but many important gaps remain.
- **3:** Identifies the candidate's actions and situation reasonably well.
- **4:** Gives clear actions, decisions, stakeholders, constraints, or trade-offs.
- **5:** Provides precise, concrete details that make the experience easy to understand.

### 4. Business Impact — Did the answer show a meaningful result?

Look for a result for customers, the business, the team, operations, revenue, adoption, quality, speed, or risk.

- **0:** No outcome is stated, or the answer is too incomplete to judge an outcome.
- **1:** Only a vague or claimed benefit, with no credible result.
- **2:** Some likely benefit is described, but it is weak or unclear.
- **3:** A credible positive result is stated, even if it is not measured.
- **4:** A clear and meaningful result is explained, preferably with evidence.
- **5:** A strong, specific, and well-supported business or customer result is shown.

**Important:** Do not invent an outcome. If the candidate says what they did but never says what changed, give a low Business Impact score.

## What not to reward by itself

Do not give a higher score only because the answer:

- Uses words such as “stakeholder,” “strategy,” or “roadmap.”
- Uses the STAR framework by name.
- Sounds confident or polished.
- Is long.
- Includes numbers that are not connected to a meaningful result.

## What to record

For every example, record:

- Relevance score
- Structure score
- Specificity score
- Business Impact score
- One short reason for each score
- Optional note: `insufficient evidence`, `ambiguous`, `contradictory`, or `fabricated claim`

Keep your notes short and based on observable words in the answer. For example: “Explains the decision, but does not say what happened afterward.”

## Example

**Question:** Tell me about a time you disagreed with an important stakeholder.

**Answer:** I disagreed with our sales lead about prioritising a custom enterprise request. I reviewed support volume and renewal risk, proposed a smaller reusable capability, and aligned engineering and sales on a two-week discovery spike. We avoided a one-off build and converted the capability into a roadmap item.

A reasonable rating might be:

| Area | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Relevance | 3.0 | Directly describes a stakeholder disagreement. |
| Structure | 3.5 | The situation, action, and result are easy to follow. |
| Specificity | 3.5 | Gives concrete actions and stakeholders, but limited detail. |
| Business Impact | 0.0 | No measured or clearly demonstrated business result is stated. |

This example is only an illustration of the rating method. Do not copy its scores onto other answers.

## Final reminders

- Rate the answer, not the person.
- Use only the evidence in the answer.
- Be consistent across all 52 examples.
- Do not change your ratings to match anyone else's.
- Honest disagreement is useful; it helps us find where the rubric needs improvement.
